Delta tells you how much the call gains when USD/CHF rises a little. With a delta of 0.50 the option's value moves about half as much as the rate.

Gamma tells you how quickly that delta itself changes. Far out of the money delta is close to zero, deep in the money close to one. Around the strike it changes fastest: that is where gamma is largest.

For the importer this means: his call on <Num of="ex.notional" /> behaves today like a forward on roughly half the amount. If the dollar rises, that share grows; if it falls, it shrinks. Gamma measures exactly this adjustment.

### Gamma in the example

With <i>S</i> = <Num of="ex.spot" />, σ = <Num of="ex.vol" />, <i>T</i> = <Num of="m05.T" /> and <i>r</i><sub>f</sub> = <Num of="ex.rf" />, <i>d</i><sub>1</sub> is about <Num of="m05.d1" />. Delta is <i>e</i><sup>−<i>r</i><sub>f</sub><i>T</i></sup> · <i>N</i>(<i>d</i><sub>1</sub>) = <Num of="m05.dff" /> · <Num of="m05.nd1" /> ≈ <Num of="m05.delta" />. For gamma the density φ(<i>d</i><sub>1</sub>) = <Num of="m05.phiD1" /> comes into play:

<Formula variant="accent"><span>Γ = <Num of="m05.dff" /> · <Num of="m05.phiD1" /> / (<Num of="ex.spot" digits={1} /> · <Num of="m05.volFrac" /> · <Num of="m05.sqrtT" />) = <b><Num of="m05.gamma" /></b></span></Formula>

If USD/CHF rises by one big figure (0.0100), delta rises by about <Num of="m05.deltaChangeApprox" digits={2} sign={false} />: from <Num of="m05.delta" /> to roughly <Num of="m05.deltaUp" />. The lab reproduces the same value numerically.

<Callout variant="good" title="Numerical check">
  Delta one pip above and one pip below spot, difference divided by two pips: Γ ≈ <Num of="m05.gammaNumeric" />. Formula and difference quotient agree. The exact delta change for a full big figure is <Num of="m05.deltaChangeActual" />, slightly less than Γ · 0.01, because gamma already fades along the way.
</Callout>

<Derivation title="Derivation in four steps">

**1.** Delta is the slope of the price curve, the first derivative of the price with respect to spot.

<Formula><span>Δ = ∂<i>V</i> / ∂<i>S</i></span></Formula>

**2.** Gamma is the slope of the delta curve. So gamma is the derivative of delta and the second derivative of the price.

<Formula><span>Γ = ∂Δ / ∂<i>S</i> = ∂²<i>V</i> / ∂<i>S</i>²</span></Formula>

**3.** In the Garman-Kohlhagen model the call's delta is Δ = <i>e</i><sup>−<i>r</i><sub>f</sub><i>T</i></sup> · <i>N</i>(<i>d</i><sub>1</sub>). Differentiating with respect to spot touches only <i>N</i>(<i>d</i><sub>1</sub>), by the chain rule. φ is the density of the standard normal distribution.

<Formula><span>∂<i>d</i><sub>1</sub> / ∂<i>S</i> = 1 / (<i>S</i> · σ · √<i>T</i>)</span><span>⇒ Γ = <i>e</i><sup>−<i>r</i><sub>f</sub><i>T</i></sup> · φ(<i>d</i><sub>1</sub>) / (<i>S</i> · σ · √<i>T</i>)</span></Formula>

**4.** Plugging in the example (<i>S</i> = <Num of="ex.spot" />, σ = <Num of="ex.vol" />, <i>T</i> = <Num of="m05.T" />, <i>r</i><sub>f</sub> = <Num of="ex.rf" />, <i>d</i><sub>1</sub> ≈ <Num of="m05.d1" />) gives Γ = <Num of="m05.gamma" />, the value from the worked example above. Gamma is measured in "delta per unit of spot": for every 0.01 move, delta changes by about <Num of="m05.deltaChangeApprox" digits={2} sign={false} />.

</Derivation>

<Desk title="Gamma versus theta">

Whoever buys an option is long gamma. Delta-hedging it, they sell after rallies and buy after dips: movement earns money. In return they pay time value every day.

<Formula variant="dark"><span>P&L ≈ ½ · Γ · [(Δ<i>S</i>)² − σ²<i>S</i>² · Δ<i>t</i>]</span></Formula>

Break-even per day: σ · <i>S</i> / √365 = <Num of="ex.spot" /> · <Num of="ex.vol" /> / √365 ≈ <b><Num of="m05.breakEvenPips" /> pips</b>. If USD/CHF moves more than the implied daily volatility, the delta-hedged gamma buyer comes out ahead. The gamma share of theta is about <Num of="m05.thetaGammaDay" /> per day; the theta in the lab (<Num of="m05.thetaDay" />) also contains the rate carry of about <Num of="m05.thetaCarryDay" /> per day.

</Desk>

<Takeaway>
  Delta is the slope, gamma the curvature. Gamma peaks around the strike, and that is exactly where each day costs the most time value.
</Takeaway>
