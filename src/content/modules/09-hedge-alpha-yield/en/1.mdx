For eight modules you have been collecting building blocks: spot, forward, swap, options, Greeks, barriers and structured products. Now we turn the question round. Not ‘What can this product do?’ but ‘What do you need?’.

Three people look at the same rate, USD/CHF <Num of="ex.spot" />:

- **The importer** has to pay USD 1m in 3 months. He wants certainty. His need is a **hedge**.
- **An investor with a market view** does not believe in a weak dollar. She wants to make money from that view: **alpha**.
- **An investor with francs in his account** wants more than the CHF rate of <Num of="ex.rd" />. He is looking for **yield**.

The need decides which risk is wanted at all. The importer already has a risk: he is short USD. He wants to get rid of it. The investor with a view wants to take a risk, but only the one she is betting on. The yield investor accepts some risk in return for more interest.

The yardstick is the same for all three: the forward. Every product in this module is measured by where it does better and where it does worse than the forward.

### Same view, different risk

Only then comes the market view. Take ‘USD will strengthen’. The importer can act on this view with a forward, a stop-loss order or a USD call. All three protect him, but each with a different worst case: a missed chance, a gap or the premium.

### Worked example: forward or call?

The importer compares two hedges for the view ‘USD stronger’. He measures the result of the hedge at expiry in pips per USD. A plus offsets his extra cost. A minus means he would have bought more cheaply without the hedge.

- **Dollar <Num of="m09.s1Move" /> stronger, USD/CHF <Num of="m09.s1Up" />:** the forward earns <Num of="m09.s1FwdUp" /> pips, so <Num of="m09.s1FwdUpChf" /> on USD 1m. The call earns only <Num of="m09.s1CallUp" /> pips, because the premium of <Num of="m09.callPremiumPips" /> pips is deducted.
- **Dollar <Num of="m09.s1Move" /> weaker, USD/CHF <Num of="m09.s1Down" />:** now it turns round. The forward returns <Num of="m09.s1FwdDown" /> pips, because the importer could have bought more cheaply. With the call only the premium is lost, <Num of="m09.s1CallDown" /> pips.

Neither is ‘better’. The forward is ideal if the view is right. The call is ideal if it is wrong. So the question is: which worst case can you bear? That is exactly what the chips in the matrix show.

<Derivation title="Derivation: why no product beats the forward for free">

**1.** In the model the rate at expiry ends up, on average, exactly at the forward: the expected value under the risk-neutral measure is the forward ([module 02](/en/module/02-forward/2/)). This is a pricing rule, not a forecast.

<Formula>E[<i>S</i><sub><i>T</i></sub>] = <i>F</i> = <Num of="ex.forward" digits={4} /></Formula>

**2.** The value of a product today is its discounted expected value. For a payoff <i>X</i>(<i>S</i><sub><i>T</i></sub>) at expiry:

<Formula>value = <i>e</i><sup>−<i>r</i><sub>d</sub><i>T</i></sup> · E[<i>X</i>(<i>S</i><sub><i>T</i></sub>)]</Formula>

**3.** A forward at the rate <i>F</i> has <i>X</i> = <i>S</i><sub><i>T</i></sub> − <i>F</i>, so its value is zero. A ‘zero cost’ product is worth zero as well. If it does better than the forward in one scenario, it must do worse in another. Otherwise it would be worth more than zero.

**4.** That is why every improvement costs something: a premium (call), part of the upside (collar) or an extra risk such as leverage and a knock-out (structured products). In practice the provider's margin comes on top.

</Derivation>

<Desk title="Three questions before any product">

Anyone comparing products should not start with the product. Three questions structure every conversation: **What is the need?** Hedging, acting on a view or earning interest. **What is the view, and how sure is it?** A direction, a range or none at all. **Which worst case can be borne, in francs?** Only once all three are answered is it worth looking at strike, coupon or interest.

</Desk>

<Takeaway>

Need first, then the view, then the product. The same view can be expressed with very different risk: always compare the worst case.

</Takeaway>
