What does the importer's right cost? The answer comes from the **Garman-Kohlhagen model**, published in <Fact id="gk-1983" />. It is Black-Scholes for currencies: with two interest rates instead of one, that of the quote currency (CHF, <i>r</i><sub>d</sub>) and that of the base currency (USD, <i>r</i><sub>f</sub>).

Six inputs set the price: spot, strike, time to expiry, vol and the two interest rates. Five of them you know: strike and time to expiry are in the contract, spot and rates come from the market. Only the vol is open, and that is exactly what the options market trades.

### The formula, all through the forward

<Formula variant="accent">
  <span><i>C</i> = e<sup>−<i>r</i><sub>d</sub><i>T</i></sup> · [<i>F</i> · N(<i>d</i><sub>1</sub>) − <i>K</i> · N(<i>d</i><sub>2</sub>)]</span>
  <span><i>P</i> = e<sup>−<i>r</i><sub>d</sub><i>T</i></sup> · [<i>K</i> · N(−<i>d</i><sub>2</sub>) − <i>F</i> · N(−<i>d</i><sub>1</sub>)]</span>
  <span><i>d</i><sub>1,2</sub> = [ln(<i>F</i>/<i>K</i>) ± ½σ²<i>T</i>] / (σ√<i>T</i>)</span>
</Formula>

Spot only enters through the forward <i>F</i> = <i>S</i> · e<sup>(<i>r</i><sub>d</sub> − <i>r</i><sub>f</sub>)<i>T</i></sup>. That is why this tool is called "Forward First".

In the example: <i>F</i> = <Num of="ex.forward" />, <i>K</i> = <Num of="ex.strike" />, σ√<i>T</i> = <Num of="m04.sigmaSqrtT" />. This gives <i>d</i><sub>1</sub> = <Num of="ex.d1" /> and <i>d</i><sub>2</sub> = <Num of="ex.d2" />, N(<i>d</i><sub>1</sub>) = <Num of="m04.nd1" /> and N(<i>d</i><sub>2</sub>) = <Num of="m04.nd2" />. The discount factor is <Num of="m04.dfd" />, because the CHF rate is <Num of="ex.rd" />. Result: the call costs <Num of="ex.callPips" /> pips.

| When … rises | Call | Put | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spot <i>S</i> | ↑ | ↓ | The forward rises. |
| Strike <i>K</i> | ↓ | ↑ | With the call you buy higher, with the put you sell higher. |
| Time <i>T</i> | mostly ↑ | mostly ↑ | More time, more possible movement. Deep in the money the interest carry can win. |
| Vol σ | ↑ | ↑ | Large moves become more likely, while the loss stays limited. |
| CHF rate <i>r</i><sub>d</sub> | ↑ | ↓ | The forward rises. |
| USD rate <i>r</i><sub>f</sub> | ↓ | ↑ | The forward falls. |

### Put-call parity

Buy a call and sell a put with the same strike. At expiry you always get <i>S</i><sub>T</sub> − <i>K</i>: above the strike the call pays, below it the put costs you. A forward at the rate <i>K</i> pays exactly the same. Same payoff means same price today:

<Formula variant="accent">
  <i>C</i> − <i>P</i> = e<sup>−<i>r</i><sub>d</sub><i>T</i></sup> · (<i>F</i> − <i>K</i>)
</Formula>

In the example the strike sits <Num of="m04.parityGapPips" /> pips below the forward. So the call is <Num of="m04.parityPips" /> pips dearer than the put: <Num of="ex.callPips" /> against <Num of="ex.putPips" /> pips. With the strike exactly at the forward, call and put cost the same.

A long call plus a short put is therefore a **synthetic forward**. Its spot delta is <Num of="m04.synthDelta" />, that is e<sup>−<i>r</i><sub>f</sub><i>T</i></sup>, and its forward delta is exactly 1: it moves one for one with the forward.

### Arbitrage when parity fails

Suppose the call is quoted <Num of="m04.arbPips" /> pips too high. Then you put on three trades:

- You **sell the expensive call** and **buy the put**. That is a synthetic short forward: at expiry you sell USD at the strike, wherever the rate ends up.
- You **buy a real forward** at the fair rate. It provides exactly the USD you have to deliver.
- Every payment at expiry is fixed today and covered by today's receipts. What remains is the pricing error: <Num of="m04.arbChf" /> on USD 1m, received today, without exchange-rate risk.

Market makers close such gaps immediately. That is why parity holds in liquid markets up to costs and spreads.

<Derivation title="Derivation: parity by replication, Garman-Kohlhagen via Black-76">

**Parity.** Portfolio A: long call and short put with strike <i>K</i>. Portfolio B: long forward at the rate <i>K</i>. At expiry, for every <i>S</i><sub>T</sub>:

<Formula>
  <span>max(<i>S</i><sub>T</sub> − <i>K</i>, 0) − max(<i>K</i> − <i>S</i><sub>T</sub>, 0) = <i>S</i><sub>T</sub> − <i>K</i></span>
</Formula>

Equal payments at expiry mean equal value today, otherwise there would be arbitrage. A forward at the rate <i>K</i> is worth e<sup>−<i>r</i><sub>d</sub><i>T</i></sup> · (<i>F</i> − <i>K</i>) today. Hence <i>C</i> − <i>P</i> = e<sup>−<i>r</i><sub>d</sub><i>T</i></sup> · (<i>F</i> − <i>K</i>). Parity needs no model and no vol.

**Garman-Kohlhagen.** Under the [risk-neutral measure](/en/glossary/#term-risk-neutral) the expected rate at expiry is the forward <i>F</i>. The model also assumes constant vol and no jumps; then the rate at expiry is lognormally distributed. The expected payoff is the Black-76 price on the forward. Discounted at the CHF rate:

<Formula>
  <span><i>C</i> = e<sup>−<i>r</i><sub>d</sub><i>T</i></sup> · E[max(<i>S</i><sub>T</sub> − <i>K</i>, 0)] = e<sup>−<i>r</i><sub>d</sub><i>T</i></sup> · [<i>F</i> N(<i>d</i><sub>1</sub>) − <i>K</i> N(<i>d</i><sub>2</sub>)]</span>
</Formula>

Substitute <i>F</i> = <i>S</i> · e<sup>(<i>r</i><sub>d</sub> − <i>r</i><sub>f</sub>)<i>T</i></sup> and you get the spot form <i>S</i> e<sup>−<i>r</i><sub>f</sub><i>T</i></sup> N(<i>d</i><sub>1</sub>) − <i>K</i> e<sup>−<i>r</i><sub>d</sub><i>T</i></sup> N(<i>d</i><sub>2</sub>). The CHF rate acts twice: it lifts the forward and it discounts. For the call the first effect wins.

</Derivation>

<Desk title="The market quotes vol, not premium">

Because spot, rates, strike and time to expiry are observable, only the vol is left to negotiate. Market makers therefore quote options in **implied vol**: the premium then follows from the formula.

In the interbank market an option is often traded together with its delta hedge. That way the parties exchange only the vol risk, not the exchange-rate risk. How the price moves with small changes is measured by the Greeks in module 05.

</Desk>

<Takeaway>

Spot and rates work through the forward; the vol is the one open input. A long call plus a short put with the same strike is a forward: C − P = DF · (F − K).

</Takeaway>
