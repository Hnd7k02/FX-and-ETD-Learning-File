The importer is free to choose the strike. A higher strike only starts protecting at a higher rate, but it is cheaper. A lower strike protects sooner and costs more. How far a strike is "in the money" is called **moneyness**.

There are two conventions. **Spot moneyness** measures the strike against spot. **Forward moneyness** measures it against the forward. This tool uses forward moneyness. A European option is only exercised at expiry, and the fair rate for that day is the forward.

An example: a USD call with a strike of <Num of="m04.kMid" /> sits <Num of="m04.kMidSpotPips" /> pips below spot. In spot moneyness it is in the money (ITM). Against the forward of <Num of="ex.forward" />, however, it sits <Num of="m04.kMidOtmPips" /> pips above. In forward moneyness it is out of the money (OTM). Same strike, two answers. So always say which rate you measure against.

In forward moneyness, as in this tool:

- **ATMF** (at the money forward): strike equal to the forward.
- **ITM** (in the money): for a call the strike is below the forward, for a put above it.
- **OTM** (out of the money): for a call the strike is above the forward, for a put below it.

### Premium = intrinsic value + time value

The **intrinsic value against the forward** is what you can already lock in today: you sell the dollars forward and exercise the call at expiry. That makes at least F − K per USD certain. So for a call the intrinsic value is DF · max(F − K, 0). DF is the discount factor that brings it back to today. The classic definition max(S − K, 0) measures the value of immediate exercise, but you cannot exercise a European option immediately. The **time value** is the rest. It pays for the chance that the rate still moves in your favour before expiry.

Three USD calls with 3 months to expiry:

- **ATMF**, strike exactly at the forward (<Num of="ex.forward" />): <Num of="m04.atmfPips" /> pips. That is pure time value.
- **ITM**, strike <Num of="m04.kItm" />: <Num of="m04.itmPips" /> pips. Of this, <Num of="m04.itmIntrinsicPips" /> pips are intrinsic value against the forward and <Num of="m04.itmTimePips" /> pips time value.
- **OTM**, strike <Num of="m04.kOtm" />: <Num of="m04.otmPips" /> pips, or <Num of="m04.otmChf" />. Again pure time value.

Against spot, the ITM call would have <Num of="m04.itmSpotIntrinsicPips" /> pips of intrinsic value and a negative time value of <Num of="m04.itmSpotTimePips" /> pips. That is exactly why the tool measures against the forward: there the time value is never negative.

Our call from section 1 has the rounded strike <Num of="ex.strike" />. That sits <Num of="m04.fwdVsStrikePips" /> pips below the forward, so it costs a touch more: <Num of="ex.callPips" /> pips.

Time value is largest at the forward and shrinks towards both wings. Deep in the money the option behaves almost like a forward; far out of the money it is almost worthless.

### Delta instead of a rate: 25Δ and 10Δ

On the desk strikes are often named by their **delta** rather than by a rate. Delta tells you how strongly the option value reacts to spot. Roughly, it is also the chance that the option ends in the money ([risk-neutral](/en/glossary/#term-risk-neutral), not a forecast). A 25Δ call therefore has about a one-in-four chance.

In the example the 25Δ call sits at <Num of="m04.call25" />, the 25Δ put at <Num of="m04.put25" />. Further out are the 10Δ strikes, <Num of="m04.call10" /> and <Num of="m04.put10" />. The advantage: "25Δ" means a comparable moneyness in every pair, at every tenor and every vol.

"ATM" also has its own meaning on the desk. It usually refers to the **delta-neutral straddle** (ATM DNS): the strike at which the call delta and the put delta cancel out exactly. It sits at <Num of="m04.dns" />, <Num of="m04.dnsGapPips" /> pips above the forward. The call delta there is <Num of="m04.dnsDelta" />, and the put delta has the same size with a negative sign.

### Pips or per cent

The premium of our call can be written in two ways. In CHF pips: <Num of="ex.callPips" /> pips, which is <Num of="ex.callChf" /> on USD 1m. Or as a percentage of notional: converted into USD the premium is <Num of="m04.callUsd" />, or <Num of="m04.callPctUsd" /> of the USD notional. Same price, different unit. So always ask how a premium is quoted.

### European or American

A **European** option can only be exercised on the expiry date, an **American** one at any time until then. In the OTC FX market the European style is standard. An American option is worth at least as much as a European one, because it gives you an extra right. In this tool all options are European.

<Derivation title="Derivation: intrinsic value, rule of thumb and strike from delta">

**Intrinsic value against the forward.** If you exercise the call at expiry and sell the dollars forward today, you receive at least F − K per USD at expiry for certain. If the rate then sits below the strike, you buy the dollars for the forward even more cheaply in the market. The certain part, discounted to today, is worth:

<Formula>
  intrinsic value = e<sup>−<i>r</i><sub>d</sub><i>T</i></sup> · max(<i>F</i> − <i>K</i>, 0)
</Formula>

**Rule of thumb for ATMF.** With <i>K</i> = <i>F</i>, Garman-Kohlhagen becomes approximately

<Formula variant="accent">
  <span><i>C</i><sub>ATMF</sub> ≈ e<sup>−<i>r</i><sub>d</sub><i>T</i></sup> · <i>F</i> · σ√<i>T</i> / √(2π) ≈ 0.4 · <i>F</i> · σ√<i>T</i></span>
</Formula>

With σ√<i>T</i> = <Num of="m04.sigmaSqrtT" /> this gives <Num of="m04.atmfApproxPips" /> pips. So the premium grows with the vol and with the square root of time.

**Strike from delta.** The spot delta of the call is e<sup>−<i>r</i><sub>f</sub><i>T</i></sup> · N(<i>d</i><sub>1</sub>). For a target delta you solve for <i>d</i><sub>1</sub> and substitute:

<Formula>
  <span><i>d</i><sub>1</sub> = N<sup>−1</sup>(Δ · e<sup><i>r</i><sub>f</sub><i>T</i></sup>)</span>
  <span><i>K</i> = <i>F</i> · e<sup>−<i>d</i><sub>1</sub>σ√<i>T</i> + ½σ²<i>T</i></sup></span>
</Formula>

**ATM DNS.** The call and put deltas cancel when N(<i>d</i><sub>1</sub>) = ½, so <i>d</i><sub>1</sub> = 0. This gives <i>K</i> = <i>F</i> · e<sup>½σ²<i>T</i></sup>, slightly above the forward.

**A neat link.** The time value of the ITM call above, <Num of="m04.itmTimePips" /> pips, is exactly the price of the OTM put with the same strike. This follows from put-call parity in section 4.

</Derivation>

<Desk title="Which ATM do you mean?">

ATM can mean spot, the forward or the delta-neutral straddle. In the interbank market, vol quotes usually refer to ATM DNS. Together with the 25Δ risk reversal and the 25Δ butterfly it describes the shape of the vol smile (module 06).

If the premium is paid in USD, as is usual in USD/CHF, the desk works with a premium-adjusted delta. The strikes then shift slightly. ATM DNS even moves just below the forward, to <Num of="m04.dnsPa" />. For every delta strike, ask which convention applies.

</Desk>

<Takeaway>

Moneyness exists against spot and against the forward. This tool measures against the forward, because a European option is only exercised at expiry. The premium is intrinsic value against the forward plus time value; with the strike at the forward it is pure time value.

</Takeaway>
