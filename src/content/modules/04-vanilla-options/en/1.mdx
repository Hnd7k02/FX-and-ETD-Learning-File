Our importer has to pay USD 1m in 3 months. With the forward from module 02 he fixes the rate today: he buys the dollars at <Num of="m04.fwdDeal" /> CHF per USD. That protects him against a strong dollar. But it also takes away every opportunity. If USD/CHF falls, he still pays the forward rate.

An option separates the good side from the bad one. It gives him the **right, but not the obligation**, to buy USD 1m in 3 months at a **strike** of <Num of="ex.strike" />. If the dollar rises above the strike, he exercises and buys at the strike. If the dollar falls, he lets the option lapse and buys more cheaply in the market.

That right has a price. The seller of the option asks for a **premium** today: <Num of="ex.callPips" /> pips, or <Num of="ex.callChf" /> on USD 1m. As with insurance, you pay the premium up front, whether or not the bad event happens. Section 4 shows where this number comes from.

### A USD call is a CHF put

In the FX market you always exchange two currencies. The right to buy USD against CHF is the same as the right to sell CHF against USD. A **USD call** is therefore also a **CHF put**.

Seen from the franc, the same option looks like this: notional <Num of="m04.chfNotional" />, strike <Num of="m04.strikeInv" /> USD per CHF. Same deal, different lens. That is why the desk always names both currencies.

### Three scenarios in numbers

With the forward the importer pays <Num of="m04.fwdChf" /> whatever happens. With the call he works it out like this: he buys at the lower of the two rates, the rate at expiry or the strike, and adds the premium.

- **The dollar rises to <Num of="m04.stUp" />.** Unhedged he pays <Num of="m04.unhedgedUpChf" />. With the call he exercises and pays <Num of="m04.capChf" />, strike plus premium. He saves <Num of="m04.saveUpChf" />.
- **The dollar falls to <Num of="m04.stDown" />.** The forward still costs him <Num of="m04.fwdChf" />. With the call he buys in the market and pays <Num of="m04.optionDownChf" /> including the premium: <Num of="m04.saveDownChf" /> less than with the forward.
- **The rate ends close to the strike.** Then the option is the dearest choice. The premium was the price of insurance that did not pay off this time.

The option caps his rate at <Num of="m04.capRate" />, strike plus premium per USD. Above that rate it beats staying unhedged. Below <Num of="m04.beatFwdRate" /> it even beats the forward. In between it is, with hindsight, the dearest choice. In return, the importer does not need to know today where the rate is heading.

<Derivation title="Derivation: the cost with a call, and the view as a CHF put">

At expiry a call with notional <i>N</i> and strike <i>K</i> pays <i>N</i> · max(<i>S</i><sub>T</sub> − <i>K</i>, 0) in CHF. The importer buys his dollars in the market at <i>S</i><sub>T</sub> and offsets the payoff:

<Formula>
  <span>Cost = <i>N</i> · <i>S</i><sub>T</sub> − <i>N</i> · max(<i>S</i><sub>T</sub> − <i>K</i>, 0) + premium</span>
  <span>= <i>N</i> · min(<i>S</i><sub>T</sub>, <i>K</i>) + premium</span>
</Formula>

The premium is paid today, the cost falls due at expiry. Strictly speaking, the premium therefore counts with interest at the CHF rate, premium · e<sup><i>r</i><sub>d</sub><i>T</i></sup>. In the example the CHF rate is <Num of="ex.rd" />, so the factor is 1.

The same option as a CHF put: notional <i>N</i><sub>CHF</sub> = <i>K</i> · <i>N</i>, strike 1/<i>K</i> in USD per CHF. It pays <i>K</i> · <i>N</i> · max(1/<i>K</i> − 1/<i>S</i><sub>T</sub>, 0) in USD. Converted into CHF at the rate <i>S</i><sub>T</sub> this gives:

<Formula variant="accent">
  <i>K</i> · <i>N</i> · <i>S</i><sub>T</sub> · max(1/<i>K</i> − 1/<i>S</i><sub>T</sub>, 0) = <i>N</i> · max(<i>S</i><sub>T</sub> − <i>K</i>, 0)
</Formula>

That is exactly the payoff of the USD call. Two names, one deal.

A note on the forward: option models work with continuous rates, <i>F</i> = <i>S</i> · e<sup>(<i>r</i><sub>d</sub> − <i>r</i><sub>f</sub>)<i>T</i></sup> = <Num of="ex.forward" />. That is only <Num of="ex.fwdDiffPips" /> pips below the money-market forward from module 02. So that forward and option stand on the same footing, this module uses the continuous forward throughout.

</Derivation>

<Desk title="How the desk talks">

In the FX options market "call" on its own is ambiguous. So you name the pair, the tenor, the strike and both currencies: "USD/CHF 3 months, strike <Num of="ex.strike" />, USD call / CHF put, USD 1m".

You also agree in which currency the premium is paid and how it is quoted, in CHF pips or as a percentage of notional. Section 3 shows how the two fit together.

</Desk>

<Takeaway>

An option is a right, not an obligation: you pay the premium today, cap your rate and keep the chance of a cheaper dollar. A USD call is always a CHF put as well.

</Takeaway>
