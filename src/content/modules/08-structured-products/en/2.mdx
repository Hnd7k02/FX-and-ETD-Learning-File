The accumulator from section 1 runs over <Num of="m08.accFixings" /> weekly fixings. On every fixing the product looks at the rate <i>S</i><sub><i>i</i></sub> and decides:

| Fixing <i>S</i><sub><i>i</i></sub> | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| at or above the barrier <Num of="m08.accBarrier" /> | Knock-out: the product ends and nothing more is bought. |
| between strike and barrier | You buy <Num of="m08.accNotional" /> at the strike <Num of="m08.accStrike" />. Gain: <i>S</i><sub><i>i</i></sub> − <i>K</i> per dollar. |
| below the strike | You buy **twice the amount** at the strike. Loss: twice <i>K</i> − <i>S</i><sub><i>i</i></sub> per dollar. |

In building blocks: per fixing, one bought USD call on <Num of="m08.accNotional" /> and two sold USD puts, all at the strike <Num of="m08.accStrike" />, all with a knock-out at <Num of="m08.accBarrier" />. The fair strike lies <Num of="m08.accBetter" /> pips below the forward of <Num of="m08.accFwd" />, with a standard error of <Num of="m08.accSe" /> pips.

### Worked example: three markets, three results

The simulator compares the product with a forward hedge. The hedge buys <Num of="m08.accNotional" /> on every scheduled fixing at that day's forward. Both are measured against the market rate. The difference shows how much cheaper (+) or dearer (−) the accumulator made the dollars than the forward did.

- **Trend up.** The dollar rises and reaches the barrier at fixing <Num of="m08.accUpKo" />: knock-out. The importer has bought only <Num of="m08.accUpUsd" />. He has to buy the rest in the market, at higher rates. Accumulator <Num of="m08.accUpPnl" />, forward <Num of="m08.accUpFwd" />, difference <Num of="m08.accUpDiff" />.
- **Sideways.** No knock-out, no fixing below the strike. Every fixing buys the normal amount at the better rate. Accumulator <Num of="m08.accSidePnl" />, forward <Num of="m08.accSideFwd" />: <Num of="m08.accSideDiff" /> for the accumulator.
- **Trend down.** See the box below.

<Callout variant="bad" title="When it goes wrong: the dollar falls sharply">

Drag the end of the path to <Num of="m08.accDownMove" />. From fixing <Num of="m08.accDownFirstLev" /> the rate is below the strike. <Num of="m08.accDownLev" /> fixings buy twice the amount, <Num of="m08.accDownUsd" /> in total. That is far more than the importer needs, bought above the market rate. Accumulator <Num of="m08.accDownPnl" />, forward <Num of="m08.accDownFwd" />: <Num of="m08.accDownDiff" />. Once a fixing is below <Num of="m08.accBreakLev" />, every double fixing loses more than the forward.

</Callout>

So the accumulator is not simply a better forward. It beats the forward when the market stays calm. In large moves it loses in **both** directions: upwards through the knock-out, downwards through the double amount.

### The decumulator: the mirror image

An exporter who has to sell dollars gets the mirror image. He sells at the strike <Num of="m08.decStrike" />, <Num of="m08.decBetter" /> pips above the forward. If the fixing is above the strike, he sells twice the amount. If the rate falls to the barrier <Num of="m08.decBarrier" />, everything ends. Building blocks: one bought USD put and two sold USD calls, with a knock-out. With the example values the decumulator is knocked out in <Num of="m08.decPKo" /> of the paths.

<Callout variant="warn" title="Risk box: accumulator and decumulator">

- **Maximum loss:** the product sets no limit. In the extreme case you buy double on all <Num of="m08.accFixings" /> fixings, <Num of="m08.accTotalMax" />, however far the rate falls. Monte Carlo: one path in 20 loses more than <Num of="m08.accQ05" />, the worst of <Num of="m08.paths" /> paths <Num of="m08.accWorst" />.
- **Leverage:** 2. Below the strike (decumulator: above the strike) you trade twice the amount.
- **Path dependence:** the knock-out ends the hedge exactly when the dollar rises, which is the scenario the importer fears. That happens in <Num of="m08.accPKo" /> of the paths, on average after <Num of="m08.accExpFix" /> fixings.

</Callout>

<Derivation title="Derivation: product against forward, fixing by fixing">

**1.** On fixing <i>i</i> the accumulator buys the amount <i>q</i><sub><i>i</i></sub> (normally <i>N</i>, below the strike 2<i>N</i>, after the knock-out 0) at the strike <i>K</i>. Against the market rate this is worth (<i>S</i><sub><i>i</i></sub> − <i>K</i>) · <i>q</i><sub><i>i</i></sub>.

**2.** The forward hedge buys <i>N</i> on every scheduled fixing at that day's forward, <i>F</i><sub><i>i</i></sub> = <i>S</i> · <i>e</i><sup>(<i>r</i><sub>d</sub> − <i>r</i><sub>f</sub>) · day<sub><i>i</i></sub>/365</sup>. Against the market: (<i>S</i><sub><i>i</i></sub> − <i>F</i><sub><i>i</i></sub>) · <i>N</i>.

**3.** Both buy what is missing in the market and sell what is too much. The difference is therefore exactly the product's cost advantage:

<Formula variant="accent">Δ<sub><i>i</i></sub> = (<i>S</i><sub><i>i</i></sub> − <i>K</i>) · <i>q</i><sub><i>i</i></sub> − (<i>S</i><sub><i>i</i></sub> − <i>F</i><sub><i>i</i></sub>) · <i>N</i></Formula>

**4.** A double fixing gives Δ = [2 · (<i>S</i> − <i>K</i>) − (<i>S</i> − <i>F</i>)] · <i>N</i> = (<i>S</i> − 2<i>K</i> + <i>F</i>) · <i>N</i>. This turns negative as soon as <i>S</i> falls below 2<i>K</i> − <i>F</i> = <Num of="m08.accBreakLev" />.

**5.** After the knock-out <i>q</i><sub><i>i</i></sub> = 0. What remains is Δ = −(<i>S</i><sub><i>i</i></sub> − <i>F</i><sub><i>i</i></sub>) · <i>N</i>: when the dollar rises, you miss exactly the gain the forward would have made.

</Derivation>

<Desk title="Three questions before signing">

How much do I buy at most, and do I need that amount at all? What happens to my hedge after a knock-out? Below which rate is a double fixing worse than the forward? Whoever knows these three numbers understands the accumulator. Whoever knows only the better strike does not.

</Desk>

<Takeaway>

An accumulator beats the forward only in calm markets. If the dollar rises, it ends too early. If it falls, you buy twice as much as planned.

</Takeaway>
